## [16:56] E14S17 Choosing a method

Let's recap how to choose a method across the whole topic. Start from what you're given. A mass gives moles by dividing by the molar mass. A gas volume at SLC gives moles by dividing by twenty-four point eight. A solution gives moles from concentration times volume in litres.
For energy: the moles of fuel times the size of delta H gives the energy released. Heating water uses m c delta T. An electrical calibration uses V times I times t, and the calibration factor is that energy divided by the temperature rise. With a calibrated calorimeter, the heat is the calibration factor times the temperature rise.
Molar enthalpy is the heat divided by the moles of the limiting reactant, with a negative sign if the reaction released heat. Efficiency is useful output over input. To find the input needed for a target output, divide by the efficiency. And whenever you compare fuels, state the basis.
