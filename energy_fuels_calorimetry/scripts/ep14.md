## E14S17 | Choosing a method
objective: Method-selection recap across the topic.
onscreen: Table "given / situation -> relationship": mass -> n = m/M; gas at SLC -> n = V/24.8; solution -> n = cV (L); fuel energy -> E = n|deltaH|; heating water -> q = mc deltaT; electrical calibration -> E = VIt, CF = E/deltaT; calibrated calorimeter -> q = CF x deltaT; molar enthalpy -> deltaH = -q/n(limiting); efficiency -> output/input; required input -> output / efficiency; comparisons -> state the basis.
transitions: Rows highlight as named.
checks: none

[b01] Let's recap how to choose a method across the whole topic. Start from what you're given. A mass gives moles by dividing by the molar mass. A gas volume at SLC gives moles by dividing by twenty-four point eight. A solution gives moles from concentration times volume in litres.
[b02] For energy: the moles of fuel times the size of delta H gives the energy released. Heating water uses m c delta T. An electrical calibration uses V times I times t, and the calibration factor is that energy divided by the temperature rise. With a calibrated calorimeter, the heat is the calibration factor times the temperature rise.
[b03] Molar enthalpy is the heat divided by the moles of the limiting reactant, with a negative sign if the reaction released heat. Efficiency is useful output over input. To find the input needed for a target output, divide by the efficiency. And whenever you compare fuels, state the basis.
